Notes on Urban Gardens
Community gardens have spread quickly through older neighbourhoods. Many began on empty lots that had been fenced off for years, and residents simply asked the owners for permission to plant.
The first harvests were small, but the gardens soon became meeting places. Older residents taught younger ones how to prepare soil, save seeds and protect seedlings from late frost.
City planners now include garden space in new housing projects. They argue that a shared garden costs little to maintain and gives people a reason to spend time outdoors with their neighbours.
Critics point out that some gardens are used only by a few families. Successful projects usually have a small committee, clear rules for each plot and an open day for visitors every month.
